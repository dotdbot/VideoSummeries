🎯 Step 1 — Find Your Niche Community

The #1 mistake: trying to appeal to everyone. Nike sells to everyone — nobody is actually loyal to Nike. A boring brand sells to one specific passionate community that big brands ignore.
  • 24/7 Running Gear — sells only to runners → $100M/year
  • Dynamic Discs — sells only to disc golfers (not even a top-50 sport) → ~$6M/year
  • Gymshark — sells only to gym-goers → $1.5B company
Why communities buy emotionally: They don't buy because of features. They buy to escape pain, fear, or insecurity. To protect people they love. To gain status and approval. To be seen a certain way by their peers. A product that taps into these emotions sells itself. Your brand needs to be something this community is emotionally desperate to have.

🔍 Step 2 — Find & Validate Your Product (4 Questions)

Use a tool like Trend Track to find trending products, then run each through these 4 filters:

Question 1
Does it solve a specific problem for a specific group?
Not just "back pain relief" — that's too broad. Think "ergonomic support for remote workers who sit 8+ hours/day." The more specific, the less competition and the higher the emotional resonance.
Question 2
Can you make 20–30% net profit after all costs?

Question 3
Is the market big enough with real emotion?
Go to Reddit and TikTok. Search for people discussing the exact problem this product solves. Are people passionate, frustrated, or desperate? Emotional conversations = a market that will buy. Flat, informational conversations = low purchase intent.
Question 4

Train an AI (ChatGPT) on your product + customer avatar. Feed it competitor links and product details. It outputs: demographics, pain points, goals, aspirations, and UGC ad prompts. Better marketing = faster proof of concept = less wasted ad spend.

The counterintuitive truth: polished, professional ads don't work in 2026. Human brains have built an immune system to traditional ads. What breaks through is content that looks like an organic post — someone talking casually to a friend. That's "ugly UGC" (user-generated content).
