You Don't Need to Start a Business. Just Buy One of These.

📅 August 15, 2026 ⏱️ ~18 min 🎙️ David — Filterbuy ($23M/mo air filters) ▶ YouTube 📄 Full Transcript
TL;DR – David runs Filterbuy (a boring air filter company doing $23M/month) and buys boring businesses for a living. He ranks business types from S-tier to F-tier based on one framework: does it have a moat, sticky customers, recurring cash flow, and no constant reinvestment? His surprising picks: grease traps, fire inspections, bookkeeping practices, and insurance agencies — all S or A tier. His biggest warnings: e-commerce, agencies, and laundromats.

🏆 Business Tier Rankings

S TIER ⭐
💧 Grease Trap / Septic Services
Every restaurant has grease traps — legally required to clean them. Compliance-driven = automatic recurring revenue. No specialty training needed; you hire the labor, manage operations. Emergency urgency = premium pricing power.

David's tip: "I'll pay you $50,000 and work for free for a year as an apprentice, then negotiate a buyout." Learn the business, then take it over. No big company competition coming after it.
S TIER ⭐
🔥 Fire & Backflow Inspection Services
Buildings are legally required to get fire and backflow inspections every year. Compliance-driven = clock-like recurring revenue. Nobody thinks about it, nobody budgets against it, nobody shops around for a cheaper option. David's own Filterbuy lost $1M in a fire and didn't get full insurance payout due to a compliance gap — that's when he learned how big this business is.

Look for: owner-operated businesses that built it but didn't professionalize it. That's the acquisition opportunity.
A TIER ✅
📊 Bookkeeping / Accounting Practices
Private equity is quietly consolidating these and using AI to make them dramatically more efficient. David knows someone personally doing this — buying 1–2 small accounting practices, systematizing with AI, then buying more and integrating them fast.

Why it works: Labor-only (no inventory, no equipment), established client relationships, and AI lets you serve far more clients per accountant. Buy with cash + owner earnout over 2 years while they help you systematize. Then acquire more.
A TIER ✅
🛡️ Commercial Insurance Agencies
Same playbook as bookkeeping. Labor-only, no physical assets. Established book of business with clients who have renewed for 5+ years. Cold calling 100 businesses for insurance is brutal — but buying an agency where they already trust you changes everything. Sticky, recurring, cash flow positive from day one.
B+ TIER
♻️ Pallet Recycling / Repair
Surprisingly large B2B business. Nobody dreams about it — that's the point. Sticky local customer base, fragmented market with no big competitors, recurring demand (those blue pallets on the highway are from pallet recycling companies). Tricky to operate but can be great if you know what you're doing.
B+ TIER
❄️ Commercial Refrigeration Services
Restaurants and stores cannot afford downtime when their cooler breaks — they lose thousands per hour. If you're the one they call, you have real pricing power. Industrial services have recurring maintenance needs plus a compliance component. Stable, growable, but tricky to operate without knowing the industry.
C TIER ⚠️
🍕 Franchises (Context-Dependent)
David's brother lost $50K on a hazmat remediation franchise — franchisor takes margins off top, you don't own the systems, not really sellable. But the Uber driver preparing to buy a Domino's franchise — who spent 3 years working in one, went through all the training, knew the exact economics — that's A tier potential. Same franchise = F tier without experience, A tier with 4 years of preparation.
C TIER ⚠️
🧺 Laundromats
The pitch is seductive — passive quarters while you sleep. Reality: machines break constantly, repair costs are hidden in P&Ls, most operators are handymen doing work themselves. Now everyone who read the same Twitter thread is competing. Can work, but the passive income narrative is mostly myth.
F TIER ❌
🛒 E-Commerce Brands
David saw this firsthand with air purifiers during COVID — massive demand, then by 2023 revenues declining and owners trying to sell lemons. Inventory risk + manufacturing risk + overseas supplier risk + algorithm risk. No moat. No protection. When the algorithm changes or cost-per-click spikes, the business evaporates.
F TIER ❌
💼 Agencies
Almost always 1–2 large clients that could churn any day. Knowledge walks out the door with the previous owner. Can be replicated in 6 months with a laptop and LinkedIn. No real moat. When David digs into agencies, he invariably finds concentration risk dressed up as recurring revenue.
F TIER ❌
🌿 Landscaping Companies
Customers switch instantly — easy for them to hire the next guy who knocks on their door. Non-competes are almost unenforceable (David lost $4.5M on an HVAC acquisition in South Florida because the seller started competing again). And if it's actually a great business? Private equity already outbid you.
"If you can afford it, it's probably not good enough. If it's good enough, you probably can't afford it."

🧠 David's Buying Framework

What to look for in an S/A-tier business:

  • Compliance or emergency need — customers can't opt out (fire inspections, grease traps) or can't afford downtime (refrigeration)
  • Established book of business — takes forever to build from scratch; buy the relationships
  • Local and fragmented — no PE firm or large competitor already dominating
  • Labor-only preferred — no inventory, no machines depreciating, no constant reinvestment → clean cash flow
  • Owner-operated but unprofessionalized — the acquisition opportunity: systematize what they built
  • AI as the multiplier — bookkeeping, insurance → same relationships, fewer people, same or better service

David's acquisition approach for small businesses:

  • Pay $50K upfront + work for free as an apprentice for 1 year
  • Negotiate an owner earnout/buyout over 2 years
  • Use that time to systematize operations and learn the business deeply
  • Then acquire similar businesses and integrate them quickly using the system

💡 Key Takeaways

  • Boring = protected. The more unglamorous, the less competition from people who watched the same YouTube video.
  • Compliance = automatic recurring revenue. Grease traps and fire inspections are legally mandated — the customer can't churn.
  • Buy the relationships, not the product. An established book of clients is worth more than any tech or system.
  • Non-competes are mostly unenforceable. David lost $4.5M learning this. Price it into your deal or don't rely on it.
  • E-commerce has no moat. Marketing-driven businesses die when algorithms change.
  • Laundromat "passive income" is a myth. You need to be a handyman or hire one — it's a job, not a cow.
  • The same franchise is F tier or A tier depending on preparation. The Uber driver who spent 3 years studying Domino's will succeed.
  • Cash flow = revenue AFTER reinvestment. A business that needs constant machine replacement isn't actually cash flowing.
