Paraguay's Ueno Bank Collapse: What Really Happened?

📺 3DUNFOLDED 📅 November 6, 2026 ⏱️ 17:10 ▶ Watch on YouTube
TL;DR: ueno, Paraguay's largest bank by customers (~2.9M, about 6 in 10 adults), went dark overnight on 3 November 2026. On Monday evening it said it was operating with "full normality". That same day the central bank's board unanimously voted to put it into resolution. According to the central bank's court filing, ueno's capital ratio was 5.56%, against a legal minimum of 12%. The bank had reported 13.8% only five days earlier. Behind it: about $146M moved to three companies linked to the owners, apparently hidden by putting the shares in the names of employees, despite at least 10 internal warnings. The central bank's president, Carlos Carvallo, had previously run ueno, and the bank got its license 12 weeks after he took office. By Thursday, President Santiago Peña had resigned and been impeached 78–0. Deposit-guarantee payouts have started. The size of the hole, and whether state and pension deposits are covered, is still unknown.

🌅 The Morning It Stopped (Tue 3 Nov)

  • José Aguilar, 37, runs an events company in Asunción. He arrives just after 7 a.m. to withdraw ~20M guaraníes (~$3,000) to pay his staff (as told to EFE).
  • Overnight the app, cards and transfers all stop, and branches stay shut. According to Última Hora, the board's decision was communicated at about 2:36 a.m.
  • A central-bank board member tells EFE: "We closed the entity. It no longer exists."
  • Depositors' stories: Jesús, a 50-year-old Cuban sports trainer, has only ~$100 of spending money, all of it on his ueno card ("I don't have cash"). A media personality whose footballer husband is paid through ueno says she doesn't know how they'll do the supermarket shopping in 15 days.

⚖️ The Three Rules That Mattered

  1. Law 861 (amended 2016): capital must never fall below 12% of risk-weighted assets.
  2. Article 59: a bank may not lend more than 20% of its capital to people or companies linked to its owners or managers. This stops insiders from quietly lending depositors' money to themselves.
  3. Law 2334 (2003): written after a decade in which, by Última Hora's count, 13 banks and 35 finance houses disappeared. If solvency falls below half the minimum, the central-bank board must open a resolution within 24 hours. It works as a tripwire.

🚩 Warning Signs and Related-Party Loans

  • June 2026: central-bank inspectors examine ueno's loans. On 8 June the supervision team files a report on related-party loans. It has not been published.
  • 10 July: bank-supervision manager Fernando Riverola writes a memo, quoted by ABC Color: the loans "once again create a situation of extreme excess over the regulatory limit." The same week, a presidential decree moves him to the board of a state development bank. No one on the record has linked the two.
  • 12 Oct (ABC Color): the bank moved $146M to three owner-linked companies between Sep 2025 and May 2026: ~$42.5M to "Mowi", ~$42M to "Grupo O'Hendi", ~$62M to "Grupo OM". AP reports nearly $39M went to the three on a single day (26 Sep 2025).
  • The 20% math: ABC puts April capital at ~$410M, so the limit is ~$82M. The linked firms owed ~$123M, about 30%. The video notes ABC's numbers don't fully reconcile, and the central bank hasn't published its own.
  • How they escaped the "linked" label: according to ABC Color, the companies' shares had been put in the names of employees and former managers, so on paper they were strangers. ueno denies this.

October denials and reassurance

  • 7 Oct: ueno's president tells Radio Monumental: "We did not know and to this day we do not know about that report", and says the bank was never asked for a regularization plan.
  • 9 Oct: the acting central-bank head says there was "probably" indirect financing and that once it's reversed, "it's cured". Peña: opening a proceeding doesn't mean the bank is taken over or will close.
  • 17 Oct: Fitch moves its outlook to negative. On Fitch's adjusted figures, capital is ~10.3%, below 12%.
  • 18 Oct: ABC Color counts at least 10 warnings about ueno inside the central bank. On 27 Oct, Peña asks for "calm and prudence" and says the system is "very delicate".
  • Thu 29 Oct: ueno announces a $100M capital raise, says its capital ratio is 13.8%, and rules out touching pension money.

🌙 The Night Everything Changed (Mon 2 Nov)

  • The central-bank board passes Resolution No. 1, Minute 49, unanimously, opening a resolution of ueno. The time of the vote has not been disclosed.
  • At 7:35 p.m., ueno issues a statement responding to rumors about the deposit-guarantee fund: the bank "continues to operate with full normality." No one has said whether the vote came before or after that statement.
  • Tuesday press conference: the central bank uses the 2003 law's wording: solvency has fallen below 50% of the required minimum. It gives no figure at the podium. Its court filing asking to freeze the owners' assets says 5.56% (ABC Color).
  • Where the 13.8% → 5.56% gap came from: "significant weaknesses in credit-risk management". More than 1.6 trillion guaraníes of loans had to be provisioned as likely losses, and AP reports expenses were booked as assets.
  • A judge freezes owners' and directors' assets: ~$70M plus more than 1.6 trillion guaraníes. Eight directors are detained, and founder/main owner Miguel Vázquez turns himself in.
  • Carvallo, who once ran ueno, announces its end and calls it a "one-off, isolated event". He says his resignation was signed in advance, and Peña asks him to go. EFE/AFP say he left Tuesday, AP says Wednesday.

💰 Where Does Depositors' Money Go?

  • Under the 2003 law, assets and debts freeze. The supervisor prepares an exclusion balance: good loans and guaranteed deposits move to healthy banks, and the rest is liquidated.
  • A bank-funded deposit guarantee fund covers up to 75 monthly minimum wages (~$38,000) per depositor. It has 15 days to pay, extendable by 15 more.
  • Law 7745 (passed Wednesday) lets the fund advance up to 40 minimum wages (~$19,800). Payments start within 24 hours and finish within 5 working days.
  • The central bank estimates ~97% of depositors are fully covered. That is 97% of people, not of money. Neither the share of deposits by value above the cap nor the coverage of state/pension deposits has been disclosed.
  • First payouts (Thu evening): balances up to 10M guaraníes (~$1,600) first. The guaraní fell more than 8% in five trading days (Bloomberg Línea).
  • History: in May 1995 the central bank took over Banco General and Bancopar at once, and depositors were paid a year late (Ámbito). Banco Unión and Banco Alemán followed. ueno is one bank, not 13, but 1995 shows how long recovery can take.

🏛️ From Bank Crisis to Presidential Crisis

  • Peña's link to ueno is disputed: AP says he was a partner in ueno's holding company until last year. Latin Times, citing filings, says his shares were cancelled in March 2025. The bank says he was never a shareholder or investor in the bank itself. A stake in the holding company isn't a stake in the bank, so these accounts may not contradict each other.
  • Wed 4 Nov: prosecutors charge 27 people, and 35 deputies file an impeachment motion.
  • Thu 5 Nov: Colorado party leader and ex-president Horacio Cartes says the party won't side with "the theft of public, private and pensioners' funds". At 1:17 p.m. Peña announces his resignation by video and apologizes "if at some point I have failed".
  • The Chamber of Deputies then impeaches him 78–0, minutes later according to AP or about an hour later according to ABC Color. That evening, deputies from Peña's own party walk out, the joint session loses quorum, and the resignation is never accepted. The impeachment proceeds, and the Senate summons Peña for 10:00 Friday. He says he will defend himself "with arguments and evidence".
  • Attorney General Emiliano Rolón opens three criminal cases covering Peña and other officials (not charges). A judge orders six held in preventive prison, including Vázquez, and two under house arrest. The charges include breach of trust and attempted fraud. All are presumed innocent. Tear gas is used outside Congress.

❓ Conclusions — What We Still Don't Know

  • The size of the hole in ueno's balance sheet, which the exclusion balance should reveal.
  • What the June inspection report says.
  • Who really stands behind Mowi, Grupo O'Hendi and Grupo OM.
  • Whether the state's own deposits, including pension funds, are covered.
  • Whether Carvallo truly stayed out of ueno decisions, since the records are unpublished.
  • How Friday's Senate session ended.

Key takeaways

  • The regulatory tripwires (12% minimum, 20% related-party cap, 24-hour resolution) existed. They failed because of hidden ownership, an unpublished inspection, a reassigned supervisor and a regulator led by the bank's former head.
  • Reported capital (13.8%) vs. actual capital (5.56%) shows how fast a "healthy" bank can disappear once loan losses are properly provisioned.
  • For depositors: deposit insurance protects most people, but large balances, institutional money and timing (1995 payouts took a year) are the real risks.
