📖 Facts — Aaron's Path

Eight failures in six months (with a full-time job)

  • AI image WordPress plugin — went viral on X, made no revenue. "Everyone loved it, no one wanted to pay."
  • AI product photos for businesses — couldn't figure out how to sell it; the tech was still early.
  • AI profile pictures — first success, about $10K in two months, until the fad died.
  • Custom AI children's books (his wife's idea) — some sales, a magazine cover and a live Good Morning Texas segment that produced not a single sale. Lesson: selling $40–60 consumer products is hard.

DocsBot: the one that stuck

  • Idea right after ChatGPT launched: at his old job he saw thousands of support tickets answering the same questions over and over.
  • Validated with a waitlist and survey: 200–300 business owners told him the core features, which became the MVP, built in about two weeks of nonstop work.
  • With only a few hundred followers, a demo post was reposted by a Japanese tech influencer: about 3 million views, hundreds of Japanese support tickets overnight, crashed servers. Sony Japan became an early customer.
  • Hit $10K MRR in about three months; his boss let him go part-time and train a replacement.
"All it takes is one dude in Japan." — Chris, on why building in public still works

💼 The Model — Selling AI Agents to Local Businesses

  • DocsBot pivoted to courting agencies and implementers: self-serve technical customers have "dried up" and competition is heavy, while small businesses need someone to implement it for them.
  • Agencies white-label or resell bots, run a pilot, then charge about $2,000 setup and up to ~$500/month maintenance. DocsBot charges the agency roughly $20–50 per agent when white-labeled.
  • The value math: a support agent costs about $40K/year (~$20/hour); a ticket takes ~5 minutes. Deflecting ~2,000 tickets a month saves thousands; the plan covers up to ~15,000 tickets/month. Example: pay $500/month, save ~$7,500 — about two full-time employees.
  • 10x rule of thumb: deliver ten times what you charge, and walk the client through the math (e.g. 2,000 tickets × 5 min, 50% deflected conservatively ≈ $7K/month saved vs. $500 charged).
  • Aaron's 19-year-old son, who doesn't code, is setting one up for a local contractor who said it could save him $100K+ a year. Chris: don't pitch him $500/month — price to the value.
  • Best niches: businesses with knowledge bases and complex product questions (e.g. manufacturers with spec-heavy manuals). DocsBot also does voice and internal (HR/Slack) bots, but 80/20 is customer support.
💡 Big picture
Aaron calls selling AI agents "the biggest business opportunity of our generation": there are about 30 million US businesses, and even OpenAI and Anthropic are paying agencies to implement AI, because great models don't reach small businesses without someone on the ground.

⚙️ Automating the Whole Process

  1. Find leads with AI: connect DocsBot's plugin to ChatGPT, Claude or Grok (a ~$20/month subscription) and ask it to find businesses with a knowledge base but no chatbot. Tools like BuiltWith show which sites use a given support widget (e.g. Help Scout) — instant niche.
  2. Find contacts cheaply: Aaron uses a pay-per-use API router (spelled "TREGT" in the transcript) that resells Apollo and SEO data at about a cent per lead, instead of paying for Ahrefs/Apollo subscriptions. Another tactic: target companies hiring support agents — they already have budget and ticket load.
  3. Build the demo automatically: the AI scrapes their help desk/docs, creates a bot and sends a demo link so they see it working on their own content.
  4. Automate the retainer: each month the AI reviews the client's stats and logs, writes a branded PDF report and emails it. "For that 500 bucks a month, it's doing it for you."

Outreach that works (best to worst)

  • In person (chamber of commerce, conferences) → phone call → cold text → email (hardest).
  • Instead of a $25K conference booth, Aaron had an AI coding agent design a ~$50 3D-printed robot as a conversation starter: 10–20 leads and at least two customers (~$2K each) from one conference.
  • Chris's tip: at $2,000 per customer, spend on unusual outreach (mail donuts, send a $1 Venmo). He claims it can lift close rates from ~0.1% to ~12% — his estimate, not data.

💡 Conclusions

  • Persistence plus fast, cheap experiments found the winner; the first eight ideas taught distribution and pricing lessons.
  • B2B with clear ROI (support tickets) sold far better than consumer novelty products.
  • The money is shifting from selling software to implementing it for small businesses — a service layer on top of cheap software.
  • AI now automates prospecting, demos and reporting, so non-technical sellers can run this with very little time per client.

🎯 Recommendations

  • Pick a niche with a knowledge base and repetitive questions; prove the savings with simple ticket math.
  • Price at roughly a tenth of the value delivered — around $2K setup and $500/month is the cited mid-range.
  • Lead with in-person and phone outreach; stand out with something memorable.
  • Let AI write the monthly value report so the retainer is easy to justify.
  • Build in public — one repost can launch a product.
