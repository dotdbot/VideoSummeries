📊 Facts & Case Studies

📺 The Old World (pre-internet)
A MASH ad reached 70 million Americans at once — bigger than the Super Bowl, weekly. Mass marketing for average products to average people worked because you had no choice.
🍝 Carmine's Restaurant (NYC, ~30 years ago)
Built-in remarkability: double portions for sharing, massive garlic, reservations for 6+ only. Result: every customer had a story to tell at work the next day — and had to recruit 4 friends to return.
🍷 Garagiste (Wine Business)
$30M/year revenue, run by 1 guy + a few assistants. Pure email list of 130,000 subscribers. Deliberately stopped taking new sign-ups — chose better, not bigger.

💡 Conclusions

1
Marketing ≠ Advertising. Marketing = Creating conditions for spread. Buying ads, hustling, interrupting people — those were tools from 1973. The internet killed them. Real marketing is making something so remarkable that people can't help but talk about it.
2
A Brand is a Promise, Not a Logo. Trust = making a promise and keeping it, especially when it's hard. If you make audacious promises and don't keep them, you're the Wizard of Oz. Trust is built one kept promise at a time.
3
Authenticity is a trap. Consistency is the goal. Customers don't want your real, messy inner self — they want the same reliable experience every time. Adopt a "professional role" the way an actor does. Your private life stays private; your brand role stays consistent.
4
False proxies destroy companies. Measuring the wrong thing (stock price, follower count, email volume, quarterly earnings) leads people to optimize for the proxy instead of the mission. What you measure in meetings is what your culture actually values.
5
Better beats louder. Less famous, more trusted. Garagiste did $30M with 130,000 emails and zero ads. You don't need more reach — you need more resonance with the right people. "When you pick your customers, you pick your future."
6
AI: use it to make things better, not just cheaper. The current wave is cost-reduction (less people, less spend). That's a dead end. The real opportunity is AI making your work more valuable — knowing your customers deeply, standing beside them, being missed if absent.
7
When AI is the buyer, you lose — unless you have brand. AI comparison-shops and picks the cheapest. If your only edge is price, you will race to the bottom. Brand, story, and trust are what make humans override the algorithm.

✅ Recommendations

1
Start with two questions: "Who do I want to help my customers become?" and "What are they hiring me to do?" If your product helps people get to where they already want to go, you barely have to push.
2
Put two numbers on your wall: (1) How many people subscribed to hear from us? (2) What % opened our last email? These are the only metrics that matter for an email-first business. Everything else is a distraction.
3
Design for remarkability — build the engine in. Like Carmine's restaurant: make something that forces people to tell others. Don't add a gimmick. Ask: "What is so unusual about this that someone would text their friend about it tomorrow?"
4
Give people something to say. People talk about things that raise their status or give them insider identity. Design your product/service so the customer feels smart, connected, or special by using it — and they'll spread the word for free.
5
Hire AI now and learn to be a good boss. For $20/month you have a squadron of eager interns. Don't wait. Upload your tool chest, your customer list, your process — teach it everything. The brand that knows you best wins your loyalty.
6
Choose "better, not bigger." Garagiste stopped taking new customers at 130K. They made more money by going deeper with the right people. Ask: can I serve 20% fewer customers 200% better? That's a more durable business.
7
Everything your company does is marketing. How you answer the phone. What you charge. What you dump in the river. If you're not involved in those decisions, you're not in charge of the brand. Marketing is a team sport — own it across the whole business.
🔧 Seth's AI Vision for Brands
Upload your tool chest to a brand's AI. Mid-project, you ask: "I can't find piece X." The AI — which has seen all your tools — says: "You don't have that tool. Want it delivered tomorrow?" The more you teach it, the more the brand is worth to you. The less likely you are to buy the cheap version on Amazon. That's the future of permission marketing.
