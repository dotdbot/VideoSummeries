🧭 Step 1 — Market Regime: Four Live Signals

Deciding whether to press, size down or flip short. He tried breadth indicators (% of stocks above their 20/50-day MA) and dropped them: thousands of slow, illiquid S&P names say nothing about fast momentum leaders.

  1. His own P&L feedback. With ~2% stops, open positions act as market probes. If 8 of the last 10 entries fail, "your own trade feedback is more accurate than any other indicator." In late August 2026 poor long feedback made him switch to the short side while indexes still looked fine.
  2. Watchlist migration. Stocks in his leading, mediocre and lagging lists are demoted step by step as they lose their moving averages. At that point nearly 50% of his names sat in the lagging list.
  3. Anchor leaders. In late June 2026 Corning (GLW) surged 40% in a week and he named it his anchor for market strength. Five days later it gapped down and closed below its 9 EMA; within 48 hours he cut six semi/hardware longs and rotated into tactical shorts.
  4. QQQ vs. IWM. Since 2008, every Nasdaq correction of roughly 15–20%+ (10+ samples) started with small-cap relative weakness. "A necessary factor but not a sufficient factor. I'm not predicting the market. I'm just going to react."

🔥 Step 2 — Leading & Weak Themes

  • Never short a laggard in a strong sector; short the weakest names in a weak sector — the more stocks look weak in it, the better. The same logic in reverse for longs.
  • Balance: about 90% chart/price action, 10% theme awareness. Example: AAOI in April 2026 — bought because the optical group led his scans, not a deep thesis; the pullback entry rode ~30% over three weeks.
  • His 2026 trades clustered in five themes: memory & semis, optical hardware, space & defense, crypto proxies, quantum computing.
  • Hong Kong time-zone edge: the US open is 9:30 pm and the close 4:00 am (hard for exits), but in his daytime South Korea, Taiwan and Hong Kong markets are open. Seeing SK Hynix roll over under its moving averages in early October gave him conviction to short US memory names (SanDisk, the leveraged SK Hynix product SKHY). Six of nine SKHY trades were small stop-outs before the October breakdown short paid 13R+.
