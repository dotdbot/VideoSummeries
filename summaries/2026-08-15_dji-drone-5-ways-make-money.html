🚁 The 5 Ways (+ Bonus)

Way #1 — His Go-To for 8 Years
Real Estate Photography $200/listing · 10 min flight
30–50 listings/month — the backbone of his income. Agents pay $200 per drone flight. Tips: target land listings (often only have cell phone shots), offer to draw approximate lot lines, promise same-day turnaround. Quick timelines are everything in real estate. Construction site documentation comes naturally from agent relationships — every 2–3 weeks, same shots each visit for easy edit comparison.
Way #2
Construction Site Documentation $3,000+/project
Visit every 2–3 weeks over a 1+ year project. Keep framing, height, and movement consistent so edits show clear progress. Works for residential subdivisions AND commercial builds. Great for builder social media — time lapses and behind-the-scenes content perform very well. Evan doesn't seek these out — they come from existing real estate contacts.
Way #3
Event Coverage $250–$500 drone · $1,000–$2,000 full video
Road races, car shows, large community events. Overhead views show crowd scale like nothing else. Often under an hour on-site for drone-only. Add a full event video (2–3 hrs of shooting) and you're at $1,000–$2,000. Sharing content online creates free advertising — attendees share it, new clients find you.
Way #4
Local Area Stock Library $2,000–$10,000/year passive
Not generic stock sites — build a deep catalog of your local area: landmarks, downtown, scenery. Sell to local agents (website headers, social media), businesses, and even production companies from across the country. Evan puts "zero effort" into this and still makes a few thousand extra a year. With focused effort: $5,000–$10,000/year. Great foot-in-the-door for bigger projects.
Way #5
Trades-Based Businesses $1,000–$1,500/day shoot
Crane companies, roofers, tree removal services, etc. Capture aerial shots of their work — but make it purposeful. Don't just document; create content they can use to get more clients. 5–10 hours of editing per project. High demand — many of these businesses have zero video content of their work.
Bonus
Airbnbs & Short-Term Rentals Higher than real estate
Unlike real estate (photos used once to sell), Airbnbs use your images for years to keep booking guests. Higher long-term value per shoot. Same approach works for boutique hotels.
Extra (more effort required)
Drone Inspections & Mapping · FPV Fly-throughs
Inspections and mapping require networking and extra technical knowledge beyond capturing images. FPV is highly niche — Evan owns a DJI Avata but doesn't offer it. In some markets, demand may justify the investment.

🛫 How to Get Started

  • Post aerial photos of your town for free — local Facebook groups, tag the town, tag tourism boards. Do this long enough and you become "the drone guy."
  • Get Part 107 certified (US) — Evan used Remote Pilot 101 course. Many free resources now available. Non-negotiable for paid work.
  • Get drone insurance — pay-per-flight options exist for beginners; yearly plans for regulars. Essential once you go paid.
  • Land listings are the easiest entry — search local land listings with weak photos, offer a discounted first shoot, add lot line overlays as a value-add.
  • Quick turnaround = more clients — promise same-day delivery. In real estate, speed matters more than perfection.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • Real estate is the most reliable, scalable drone income — 30–50 shoots/month is very achievable.
  • Local stock library is underrated: zero ongoing effort, steady passive income, great door-opener.
  • The best clients come from relationships, not cold outreach — construction sites flow from existing real estate contacts.
  • Adding video production to any gig (events, trades) 3–5x the revenue per client.
  • Airbnbs > real estate per shoot in long-term value (content used for years, not once).
  • Build your local brand first (free posts) → clients come to you → scale from there.
