☕ The Café Analogy

  • Open House (Bali): queues outside, food, coffee, merch, even a sauna and ice bath. People come for breakfast and return for dinner; it has expanded to three locations.
  • Nearby cafés with good coffee and cool spaces sit empty and close within months. Opening a café is easy — just like launching a website now.
  • The internet: websites grew by ~118M between January and July (~650K a day). Of ~1.5B sites, ~85% are inactive (parked, abandoned) and roughly 1 in 100 live sites makes real money.
"85 dead, 14 brochures, one business." — a high street where 85 shops are boarded up, 14 have a window display with nothing to buy, and one is open.

😩 Three Common Website Problems

  1. "My site's out of date and I'm scared to touch it" — upkeep. Agencies charge $100–150/hour and owners are busy, so the site is abandoned, not beaten.
  2. "It looks beautiful but brings no clients" — the digital brochure. 82% of US small businesses have a site that just describes them (home, about, services, contact) with nothing to buy.
  3. "When I stop posting, the leads stop" — "digital homelessness": renting a stall in someone else's shopping center.

🏢 Extremistan and Digital Landlords

  • From Nassim Taleb: in Mediocristan no one moves the average (height); in Extremistan one outlier dominates (wealth). The internet went from a high street of small sites to a few giant shopping centers.
  • Only ~360 of every 1,000 US Google searches result in a click to an external site; nearly 6 in 10 end without a click. AI gives one answer with a few citations — it picks the winners.
  • Amazon as landlord: sellers paid ~$172B in fees plus ~$68B in ads last year — roughly $200B in "rent". Rules and algorithms can change overnight; one of his clients had her account restricted.
  • You don't own your followers, data or relationship. Platforms are roads that lead past your door — rent them happily, but don't live in a tent on the road.
  • Digital sovereignty: own the keys and the customer data.

🚪 Collection Points

  • From his mentor Paul Counsel: people will spend their money anyway; a collection point is a way for them to spend it with you. Coffee is one point, food another, art, merch, sauna, drinks — Open House has 30+; empty cafés have 4–5.
  • Most small-business websites have zero: no offer to buy, no free resource, no way to stay in touch.
  • Only about 1 in 4 customers come back to buy again (2024 study of 1,500+ Shopify stores) — so you need more doors for the same customer, not just repeat purchases.

Three questions for your site

  1. What can someone buy from you today, straight from your website?
  2. What can someone not ready to buy take for free in exchange for staying in touch?
  3. What would you sell next to someone who already bought?

Can't answer all three? You've found your next collection point.

🎯 Doesn't This Contradict "One Offer"?

  • Hormozi's "one offer, one avatar, one channel" is right about focus: one audience, one problem. Selling coffee and car insurance would be chaos.
  • But everything the café sells is for the person already at the table. Even Hormozi's newer book $100M Money Models describes a sequence of offers to the same customer.
  • AI makes extra doors cheap: an idea on Monday can have a landing page, copy and email sequence live by Friday. BraveBrand added an events offer this way, mostly built by his AI agent team.
  • Online collection points: lead magnet, quiz funnel, newsletter, events, community, offers, services.

The value ladder (Russell Brunson)

  • Offers at rising price points toward the same goal: free → small → course → community → program → top-tier offer.
  • BraveBrand: free content → $14 community → up to $100,000. Most people stay at the bottom, and that's fine — each rung is another chance for a yes.

🧪 Small Experiments (Antifragile)

  • Taleb: individual restaurants are fragile, but the restaurant scene is antifragile because failures teach the survivors. "If no restaurant could ever fail, we'd all be eating cafeteria food."
  • Be the whole food scene, not one restaurant with one bet. If new smoothies flop, the café drops them and carries on.
  • Because data flows back to one place you own, each failed test makes the other offers smarter. Having a "bread and butter" offer makes flops emotionally easier.

🤖 How Businesses Get Recommended by AI

  • AI works like a friend recommending a café — it names one place. It looks at how widely and well your brand is mentioned across sites.
  • Ahrefs study of 75,000 brands: the strongest predictor of appearing in Google's AI answers was brand mentions across the web, not backlinks.
  • YouTube is one of AI's biggest citation sources — be present on YouTube, LinkedIn, Instagram, X.
  • What to publish: answer the questions customers ask on sales calls, emails and DMs — publicly, in depth, in many formats — to build topical authority.
  • Websites still matter: even after a recommendation, people do due diligence — is the site current, does the copy speak to me, are there resources? Put FAQs on your site (AI looks for them).
  • AI-referred visitors convert best: 5.8%, the highest of any channel (Ruler data).

🏠 Inside BraveBrand's Digital Home

  • Hallway = homepage: social proof, case studies, FAQs.
  • Rooms = single-purpose pages: e.g. their event page with one call to action — sold out the event with no public external marketing.
  • Roads in: YouTube (value content), Instagram (clips pointing to YouTube), a Skool community as another collection point — all linking back to the website.
  • The $35K client: a Netherlands company with no prior contact asked ChatGPT for a boutique branding agency; BraveBrand came up. They visited the site, booked a call, and Luke closed the deal.

💡 Conclusions

  • Most websites don't make money because they were never built to — they're brochures.
  • The difference between the empty café and the packed one isn't the coffee; it's how many ways people can spend money and whether they have a reason to come back.
  • The system amplifies the person running it: "AI can give you a Formula 1 car, but you need to learn how to drive it." Build yourself alongside the system — there's no shortcut.
