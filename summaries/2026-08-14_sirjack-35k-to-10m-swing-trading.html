⚙️ Kevin's Swing Trading Rules

❌
No margin, no options, no crypto, no leverage Products that can go to zero overnight are off the table. Worst case in a legit stock = 20% drop on earnings. That's survivable. Zero overnight is not.
🎯
Go all-in on ONE stock at a time "Concentration makes wealth. Diversification keeps it." (Warren Buffett). Full port = fully invested, fully tracking every move. Even $20 in a stock makes you obsessed with it.
🚫
Never chase the hot stock By the time you hear about it, it's already run up. Support is gone. You're buying the top. Kevin never bought Tesla or Nvidia — great companies, but he doesn't chase.
📊
Buy near support, look for catalysts Worst case: it bobs sideways. Best case: catalyst hits, it runs 20%+, you sell and repeat. "Buy the rumor, sell the news" — buy ahead of earnings/announcements, ride the runup.
📣
Trade sentiment, not fundamentals Never run a DCF in his life. Reads X and Reddit all day to understand where sentiment is heading. "I'm a vibe trader." The market is sentiment — predict where sentiment goes, not earnings.
📖
Look for a story + catalyst combo Needs both: (1) Good long-term company narrative — not a meme that pops tomorrow. (2) Short-term catalyst that could fire any day — deal announcement, partnership, earnings beat, sector tailwind.
📈
Watch volume closely Increasing volume = more market participants entering = more room for growth. One of his key signals. A spike + volume = real move. A spike without volume = suspect.
🛑
Mental stop-losses, not systematic If down 20–30% and the chart looks ugly, he exits. No hard stop-losses set in the system — pure discipline. Never sold a stock within 3 days of buying. Lets the thesis play out.
📱
X (Twitter) is the new CNBC All-day presence on X. Real traders post trade receipts and logic with their name attached — more valuable than anonymous Reddit. His primary edge is reading real-time sentiment faster than others.
😴
Don't force a trade If the thesis isn't strong enough, sit in cash. The RCAT mistake happened partly because he felt pressure to perform. "If you're trying to force it, just cool down."
