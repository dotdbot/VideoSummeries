1️⃣ ENVIRONMENT — Market Structure + GEX

All done before market opens. Never during live price action.

Market Structure (1H / 4H charts)

  • Identify regime: Value Up (higher highs, higher lows), Value Down, or Sideways
  • Where is value being created? Where is the market searching for new value?
  • Check prior week + current week — sets the directional bias

GEX — Gamma Exposure (Tanuki Trade)

  • Positive Gamma (+): Dealers sell rips, buy dips → volatility dampened → failed breakouts, choppy. Retail breakout strategies get destroyed here.
  • Negative Gamma (-): Dealers buy rips, sell dips → volatility amplified → bigger, faster directional moves. Order flow edge is strongest here.
  • Key levels: Call Wall (705 zone in example), Put Wall (788/886), Gamma Flip Zone — the line between regimes
  • Uses Naive GEX for NQ/QQQ (CBOE data = SPX only, costs $300/mo)
Whiteboard example: Value Up structure + Negative Gamma = expect large fast moves upward when triggered. Pre-market setup complete before clicking any buttons.
"I will never do this while price is moving a million miles an hour. I need to build my scenarios before the market actually opens."

2️⃣ LOCATION — Where to Do Business

Value Area + Discount / Premium

  • Value Area: Range where most volume was accepted — buyers and sellers "agree"
  • Discount: Below Value Area → favorable to BUY. Premium: Above → favorable to SELL.
  • In Value Up structure: only look for longs in discount. Never sell in premium into trend.

Low Volume Nodes (LVNs)

  • Asia session / London session often creates fast inefficient moves through areas with little volume
  • Those gaps = Low Volume Nodes = price will be "attracted back" to fill them
  • When NY open starts dropping back into a LVN in discount → prime location
⚠️ "When price drops to discount and breaks VAL — do NOT sell it there. That is exactly the most optimal area to buy. Location tells the direction; order flow tells the timing."

3️⃣ CONFIRMATION — Order Flow + Delta

Zoomed into 5-minute MNQ candles. Two overlays: Volume Profile + Delta Profile per candle.

The Absorption Pattern

  • Phase 1: Price in discount. Sellers aggressive. Candle = large wick down, negative delta at low, POC at bottom.
  • Phase 2: Candle closes bullish despite seller aggression → sellers got NO result for their effort.
  • Phase 3: Next candle opens up, sellers try again — but fail at a higher level. Failure of sellers.
  • Phase 4: Candle flips bullish, 400%+ imbalance of aggressive buyers fires on bid×ask ladder. → GO LONG.

The Bid × Ask Imbalance System

  • Right side (Ask) = aggressive buyers. Left side (Bid) = aggressive sellers.
  • Indicator bolds numbers when imbalance ≥ 400% on one side.
  • Bold buyers lighting up after seller absorption = confirmed dominance shift → entry trigger.
"I use order flow mainly to filter trades OUT. Some days I take zero trades. Order flow shouldn't make you take more trades — it should make you take fewer, better ones."

4️⃣ ENTRY / EXIT / RISK MANAGEMENT

  • Entry: After second seller failure + buyer imbalance confirmed. No FOMO, no anticipation, no premature entry.
  • Stop: Below the structural level that defined the setup. Prefers confirmation over a better entry price.
  • Targets: Previous swing highs/lows (primary). POC or Call Wall (secondary). Trail stop as price enters resistance.
  • Result: 1.5–2R average. Not chasing 10R — "I want to be right, not look cool."
Prop firm math on the whiteboard: $50K account → $2,000 drawdown limit / $3,000 profit target = literal 1.5R. At 65% win rate = consistent funded payouts. Simple and repeatable.

🚫 Filters — When NOT to Trade

  • Volume below 20,000 contracts / 5-min MNQ candle → participation dying, skip. Lunch hour territory.
  • After 90 minutes from NY open → hard cutoff. Data shows dramatic quality degradation after this.
  • No confirmation in location → being in discount isn't enough; must see seller absorption + dominance shift.
  • After 2 consecutive losses → stop. Probability of C-game session spikes sharply.
  • Balanced / choppy market → buyers and sellers comfortable, no forced participation = no edge.
  • News event at the level → skip the setup entirely.
"Your biggest advantage as a trader is selective participation. Most traders don't use it — they think if you're not trading, you're doing something wrong."

🧠 Psychology — A/B/C Sessions & Good vs Bad Losses

Three Session Types (Unrelated to P&L)

  • A-Game: Executed exactly per plan — no hesitation, no FOMO, no chasing. Even a losing A-game session is a success.
  • C-Game: Rules bent, oversized, stopped out, tilted, chased. One C-game session can erase a week of A-game profits.
  • Key insight: Consistency = eliminating C-games. You can't control how many great setups appear. You CAN control the dumb losses.

Good Loss vs. Bad Loss

  • Good loss: Valid setup, proper execution, market said no. Accept it — variance is real.
  • Bad loss: You guessed (entered without confirmation), anticipated, forced a trade. Even if it occasionally wins = reinforcing wrong behavior.
  • Bad loss → frustration → rule-bending → tilt → account gone. The prop firm "boom and bust" loop.

Chris's Specific Rules (Discovered via Trade Data)

  • Hard stop: 90 minutes after NY open — his trades "get dramatically worse" after this point.
  • Hard stop: 2 consecutive losses — 50/50 chance of bad decisions from loss #3 onward.
  • Premature entry or chase = immediate shutdown, no exceptions.
  • Motivation: "I think back to the version of me waking at 4:30 AM and losing every day. I never want to be that person again."
"If you're struggling, size all the way down to the point where it's almost insulting. Now you can focus on actually executing properly — not on P&L screenshots."
