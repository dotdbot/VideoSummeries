What Is RH?

  • Very high-end luxury furniture company — not your average home goods store. Their stores are architectural landmarks, often with fine dining restaurants on the rooftop to draw foot traffic and build brand association.
  • The food isn't the profit center — it's a relationship tool. People come for dinner and walk away connected to the brand even if they're not yet customers.
  • Target customer: multi-millionaires, top 10% of wealth — people furnishing $5M–$20M homes who might spend $500K+ just on furniture for a single property.
  • Jeremy's personal example: spent $5–7K furnishing a 2,700 sqft rental in 2017. Later, furnished a $3M home for $150,000. Now spends even more. A buyer of a $20M home nearby would likely spend $500K+.

Why Jeremy Loves the High-End Business Model

  • The "one customer = $2M" principle: A mass-market casino needs 10,000 customers losing $200 each to make $2M. A luxury casino (like Wynn Resorts, which Jeremy owns) can make $2M from a single whale. Same math applies to furniture.
  • LVMH comparison: Selling a $4,000 handbag to one customer vs. a competitor needing 100 customers at $40 each for the same revenue — with far better margins.
  • Ferrari vs. GM: Ferrari makes $100K+ profit per car. GM makes $2K (or loses money). If Jeremy had to own any automaker, it would be Ferrari, every time.
  • Rich customers are recession-resistant: The wealthy spend regardless of economic conditions. This provides a natural floor to demand.

The Problem — Valuation Math Doesn't Work

🔴 Valuation Problem
  • Market cap: $426 billion
  • Forward PE: 100+
