🎯 The Playbook — What Made It Work

  • New formats win. Don't compete in existing formats — create a new one. Nicotine-format energy pouches, blunt formats, gummy packs — whoever creates the new form factor has no competitors. This is the #1 highest-odds path to success.
  • LTV:CAC discipline. From his Summit Partners PE background, Chad knows LTV:CAC ratios for hundreds of brands. His benchmark: 3x minimum to be acquirable. Best he saw (COVID era): 4–5x. LTV = 36-month fully burdened gross profit (product cost, discounts, returns, fulfillment, shipping, merchant fees). CAC = cost to acquire.
  • Supplements require retention. Unlike hard goods (bed sheets, furniture), supplements don't make money on day one — you need retention revenue. This is an extra lever but also an extra risk. You burn capital upfront and make it back on months 2–36.
  • Inspiration: Doctor Squatch. Bar soap brand that sold to Unilever for $1.5B. Same playbook: take a boring everyday product (soap), make it aspirational, brand partnerships (Harry Potter, Star Wars, SpongeBob), make consumers feel like the brand reflects who they are. Grüns did this for supplements.
  • Ad funnels per angle. One landing page per ad theme — gut health, energy, immunity, etc. Each page only needs a headline swap and a few block changes. Use Replo (Shopify plugin) to build hundreds of landing pages cheaply. This gives incremental performance vs. a general page.
  • PE-level forecasting. Chad met with thousands of entrepreneurs at Summit Partners. He could forecast his own outcome from day one based on the data patterns he'd internalized. Didn't model past $100M revenue — "I'm not a sociopath." But he tracked exactly to his internal forecast.
