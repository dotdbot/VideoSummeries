🎯 The 5-Step Playbook

Step 1
📉 Demand-Based Pricing (Undercharge First)
Brett launched Design Joy at $449/month — objectively too cheap for unlimited design. But the genius was speed: he got his first paying client in under 24 hours. The goal wasn't profit, it was reps. He put his ego aside and delivered thousands of designs, becoming world-class in the process. As demand grew, so did prices:

$449 → $1,000 → $3,000 → $5,000 → $8,000/month per client

Today, 16 clients at $5,000 = ~$1M/year. The undercharging phase built the credibility and skill that justified the premium.
"Instead of waiting for someone to pay him $5,000 a month, he threw himself into the fire. These reps turned Brett into a world-class designer."
Step 2
🚧 Bold Boundaries (Protect Your Time)
At peak demand, Brett was working 16-hour days toward burnout. Instead of hiring, he created two non-negotiable rules:

Rule 1: All requests submitted asynchronously via a Trello board — no calls, no meetings, everything in writing.
Rule 2: One active request at a time. Client must wait for delivery before submitting the next one.

Result: clients spend more time thinking through requests → fewer total requests → less back-and-forth → Brett gets days to deliver quality work → everyone wins. Some clients leave. The right ones stay.
Step 3
💰 The $5,000 Client (Not $500)
Two types of clients exist. $500 clients: cheap, nagging, ask for refunds, churn in a month, don't make much money themselves. $5,000 clients: rich, don't bother you, always pay, stay long-term.

Step 4
⚡ High Demand, Low Touch Product
The reason Design Joy works as a solopreneur business: website design is built once, rarely revisited. Brett can deliver a world-class landing page in a couple of hours (years of experience). He works 6 hours/day across 16+ clients because the service is low-touch per client.

Compare: a productized bookkeeping or video editing service would be far less profitable — both are high-touch (ongoing daily/weekly work), more costly to fulfill, and less differentiated. Brett chose a service that is simultaneously:
✅ High impact (a website is a startup's first impression — the difference between winning and losing)
✅ High demand (every company needs design)
✅ Low touch (build once, done for months)
Step 5
♾️ Build Once, Sell Forever (Passive Income Stack)
In 2019, Brett spent 6 hours creating Scribbles — a $4.99 downloadable template pack for designers. It sold 25,000+ times. Then he built a course: Productize Yourself — teaching other designers to replicate his model.

Today, info products = 29% of his $1.8M revenue. He earns this without trading hours, because the work was done once. This also acts as an insurance layer — if Design Joy slows, the passive income keeps flowing.

Key: He still runs Design Joy and serves clients. The passive layer supplements, it doesn't replace the core craft.

💡 Key Lessons

  • Undercharge first — speed and momentum beat perfection. Get reps, build skill, then raise prices.
  • Boundaries are not rudeness — they are the infrastructure that makes solopreneurship sustainable.
  • $5K clients are easier than $500 clients — they pay reliably, don't churn, and don't drain your energy.
  • Pick a high-demand, low-touch service — not every productized service works. Graphic design for landing pages is uniquely suited.
  • Monetize your expertise twice — once in your service, once in info products that teach others your method.
  • Async everything — no calls, no meetings. Writing forces clarity on both sides.
  • Stay solo — hiring dilutes profit and adds management overhead. AI + boundaries + right clients = no need for a team.
